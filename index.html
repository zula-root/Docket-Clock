About this project

Sentence Clocks

Three clocks that tell the time in sentences. One uses train schedules, one court cases, and one rocket launches.

What they are

The Transit Clock shows a real train, ferry, or bus scheduled to leave at the current minute, somewhere in the world. You can choose what part of the world to look at.

The Docket Clock shows a sentence from an American court case that mentions the current time. Its grid is the whole day. Each dot represents a minute. The bigger the dot, the more often that minute turns up in the record.

The Space Clock shows a real rocket that lifted off at the current minute, by the clock at its launch site. Where no rocket has, it shows something an Apollo astronaut said at that minute, or a research rocket.

What I did

  1. Gathered the data. Train schedules from about thirty transit networks, 145,000 sentences that mention a time from Harvard’s collection of American court cases, and a public log of rocket launches.
  2. Picked a sentence for every minute. Anything about violence, illness, or children was left out.
  3. Designed and published them. Each clock is one web page, hosted on GitHub Pages.
