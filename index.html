About this clock

A clock made of court opinions.

Each minute it shows a sentence from a real, published American court opinion that names that exact minute: a burglar alarm, a milk truck, a jury still out. The words are the court’s own.

The grid is the whole day, one dot a minute. The bigger the dot, the more often that minute turns up in the record. Two minutes have never come up at all.

The idea is borrowed from the literary clock, which tells the time with sentences from novels. The opinions come from Harvard’s Caselaw Access Project, which put American case law online for anyone to read.

One sentence is shown for each minute, and each was read before it went in.
